Claude visual explainers

These demos show educators that Claude Opus 5.5 has made it possible to create good-quality visual explainers using code alone to break down complex ideas. The examples given are taken from philosophy, maths and economics. Because every picture, word and timing is written in code, rather than generated by a video or image model, it’s easier to keep the content accurate and consistent, and to change one detail without starting again. Each explainer comes in three forms, so you can choose the one that suits you.

Interactive explainer

Go at your own pace, section by section, and try things with the controls. Opens in your browser, with nothing to install.

Video

Watch from start to finish, or download it to share or add to a course page. There’s no narration: all the words are on screen.

Text version

Everything in words, in an accessible PDF, for anyone who can’t watch or use the explainer.

Philosophy

The panopticon

An interactive model of Bentham’s prison design and the idea of power through constant, unseen observation. Build the model step by step, then turn it, cut it open and see it from a cell or from the tower.

Maths

The rice and the chessboard

The old story of doubling grains of rice on each square, used to explore how quickly exponential growth gets out of hand. Make a prediction, then move across the board square by square, with a chart and sliders to experiment with.

Economics

The tragedy of the commons

A short animated film, in a risograph print style, about how shared resources get used up when everyone acts in their own interest, and how communities have kept them going. It ends with a class brainstorm and questions for pair discussion.
