The tragedy of the commons

A one-minute riso-print film on why shared resources get overused – and why they don’t have to be. With captions and sound.

A demo, not a lesson

This film shows how educators can use Claude to make animated teaching resources. The tragedy of the commons was chosen simply as an example topic, and all the content should be treated as unchecked dummy content.

Every picture, movement, note and bleat is code, written by Claude Opus 5.5 (on high effort) in an ordinary Claude chat, from the director’s prompts. No drawing, animation or audio software, stock images or recorded sounds were used.

    Pair discussion

    After the class brainstorm at the end of the film, pause for these questions in pairs.

    1. What did each herder gain from one more sheep – and what did everyone lose? Why is that sum lopsided?
    2. Who, if anyone, is to blame for the collapse? Would a better-informed herder have acted differently?
    3. What makes an agreed limit stick? Who checks it, and what happens to someone who cheats?
    4. Hardin’s pasture had no rules at all – it was really open access. Real commons usually have rules. Does that change the lesson?

    Further reading

    William Forster Lloyd, Two Lectures on the Checks to Population (1833).

    Garrett Hardin, “The Tragedy of the Commons”, Science 162 (1968), 1243–1248.

    Elinor Ostrom, Governing the Commons (Cambridge University Press, 1990). Ostrom shared the 2009 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences for this work.
